I am a mechatronics engineer and early-career researcher with 
research interests in adaptive control, learning-based robotics, 
and safe autonomy. Specifically, I am interested in how 
reinforcement learning and data-driven methods can produce 
controllers capable of adapting reliably to real-world uncertainty.

I hold a Bachelor of Engineering in Mechatronics Engineering 
from the [University of Benin](https://uniben.edu/), where I 
graduated as the Best Graduating Student in my class of 100 
students with First Class Honours, becoming the first student 
in the department's history to achieve this distinction. I am 
currently preparing to apply for fully funded PhD positions in 
robotics, with a particular interest in research groups working 
at the boundary of classical control theory and modern machine 
learning.

I am an incoming Research Assistant with the Department of 
Computer Science at the [University of Lagos](https://unilag.edu.ng/), 
working under the supervision of 
[Dr. B.A. Sawyerr](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=xqAdWOYAAAAJ&view_op=list_works&sortby=pubdate). 
My work there will focus on artificial intelligence and robotics, 
building toward publications that strengthen my research profile 
ahead of my PhD applications.

My interest in the sim-to-real gap, the persistent challenge 
of deploying controllers that perform reliably in the physical 
world after training in simulation emerged from direct 
experience. During an embedded systems internship at Chestnut 
& Bolt Tech, I identified a reliability failure in a pick-and-place 
robotic arm caused by minor variations in lighting and object 
friction that never appeared in simulation. My collaborative work 
on the "Design of a Mini-Autonomous Vehicle for Smart Mobility 
Solutions" further reinforced this, when our prototype struggled in 
real-world testing due to environmental noise despite reasonable 
perception accuracy in controlled conditions. These experiences 
fundamentally shaped my focus on robustness, uncertainty-aware 
decision-making, and the practical limits of learned controllers 
outside idealized environments.

My [projects](/portfolio/) 
reflect this trajectory. I recently implemented and evaluated 
the Bug2 reactive navigation algorithm from scratch in ROS2 and 
Gazebo, including full trajectory recording, quantitative metrics 
analysis across multiple trials, and RViz path visualization. 
This work serves as a foundation for my ongoing efforts toward 
replacing the Nav2-based local planner (DWA) with a 
reinforcement learning-based policy. I have also worked on 
simulation-based design of a 6-DOF robotic arm for autonomous 
maritime cargo handling, and contributed to computer vision 
pipelines for anomaly detection in critical infrastructure 
using aerial imagery.

Teaching and mentorship have run alongside my research 
throughout. I spent time as a Teaching Assistant at the 
University of Benin, supporting undergraduate instruction in 
robotics simulation and control fundamentals, and I have 
informally tutored students in core engineering courses for 
several years. I also introduced over 300 high school students 
to robotics and Arduino programming through a STEM mentorship 
program at Chrisidia Education Center, because I believe that making 
robotics accessible and locally relevant in Africa is as 
important as the research itself.
